Why revenge trades are so expensive

When you lose, cortisol spikes and your brain shifts into recovery mode. You stop evaluating setups rationally and start hunting to recover your loss. You ignore signals you'd normally respect, size up beyond your plan, and move faster than the market allows.

"I knew I shouldn't have entered. I was still angry from the previous trade. But I did it anyway — and lost ₹22,000 in 8 minutes."

— Neel R., options trader, Pune

Hope is not a stop-loss

Loss aversion makes losses feel twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good. We hold losers far too long, telling ourselves "it'll come back," while cutting winners early to lock in the feeling of profit. F&O options traders are especially exposed — time decay works against holding losers.

"I held BANKNIFTY PE from ₹280 down to ₹18. I kept averaging. I told myself it would reverse. It didn't."
