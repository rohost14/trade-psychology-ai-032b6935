Four boxes in a row is a summary. Three figures stacked behind hairlines is a reading. Use the grid when the metrics are independent, and the stack when they belong to one story and the reader is meant to take them in order.

Verdict

This week

You followed your plan on 9 of 12 trades.

The three you did not were all after a loss, and together they cost ₹12,400.

The serif exists for this. A verdict is a sentence, not a metric, and setting it in the display face is what separates “here is your data” from “here is what happened”. Used once per screen at most.

How money is written

Do +₹4,200.50 — sign always shown on a change.
−₹1,240.00 — a true minus sign, not a hyphen.
₹1,45,000 — Indian grouping: 1,45,000 not 145,000.
₹45,200 — paise dropped on period totals.
₹4,200.50 — paise kept on a single trade.
Don’t 4200.5 — unformatted, unsigned, unreadable at a glance.
₹4.2k — never abbreviate money the reader might reconcile.
(1,240) — accounting parentheses. This is not a ledger for accountants.
₹0 in green — zero is not a gain. Zero is ink.
