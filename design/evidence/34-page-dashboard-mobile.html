What changes, and what does not

The verdict moves inside the P&L card. On desktop it has its own surface. At 390px a second card for one sentence pushes the positions below the fold, so it becomes the closing line of the block it belongs to — still the serif, one size smaller.

The table becomes two-line rows. Six columns cannot survive 390px. Symbol and quantity stack on the left, P&L and change stack right-aligned — the two things being compared stay in a column, which is the property that mattered about the table in the first place.

Four metric cards become two. Session P&L is promoted into the hero; trade count folds into its subtitle. Only loss budget and exposure keep their own surface, because they are the two a trader checks mid-session without reading anything else.

Nothing is hidden behind a tap. Every figure on the desktop page is still on this one. Mobile changes the arrangement, not the content — a shorter mobile version is a different product, not a smaller one.

No floating action button. A raised centre button implies one dominant action per screen. These screens are for reading, so the bar stays flat and all five targets are equal.
