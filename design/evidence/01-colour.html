Colour

The ground is warm paper, not off-white grey. That single change is what makes the system read as a written record rather than a default web application — and it is what oxblood and forest green were chosen against.

Three ground levels

inset
#EEEAE1

Wells, table headers, anything pressed into the page.

paper
#F6F3ED

The ground. Everything sits on this.

raised
#FFFDFA

A surface that has earned separation. Warm white, never pure.

Three levels, so depth comes from the ground rather than from shadow. Two levels — a grey page and a white card — is the arrangement every web app already has, and it is why a two-level system reads as basic no matter how good the type is. The third level lets a table header or an inset well recede without needing a border to say so.

Dark is specified, not inverted. Oxblood goes muddy and forest green disappears against a dark ground, so both money colours are lifted and desaturated. The ground itself sits at #1B1B22 rather than near-black: a true void has no surface for the eye to rest on, and every card starts to glow. Note also that a solid severity pill in dark takes dark text — white on #E5697C fails contrast.
