Charts

A chart here is a figure that happens to have a shape. It follows the same rules as the rest of the system: monospaced ticks, hairline grid, and colour reserved for money.

The line is accent, not green. An equity curve is a shape over time, not a single gain — colouring it green would spend the money colour on something that is up today and down tomorrow. Green and red are reserved for figures that are definitively one or the other.

Signed bars

P&L by weekday

Here the colour is earned — each bar is a settled figure that is either a gain or a loss, so up and down apply directly.

The zero line is solid and slightly darker than the grid. It is the only reference that matters on a signed chart, and a reader should never have to work out which gridline it is.

Axes & grid

Ticks are monospaced so the y-axis has a fixed width and the plot does not shift when values change.
Grid is dashed hairline at #E5E3DE, horizontal only. Vertical gridlines imply the x-axis is continuous when it is usually categorical.
Currency is abbreviated on axes (3k, 9k) but never in a figure the reader might reconcile against their broker.
No axis lines except zero. The ticks are enough.

Dark on light — a tooltip is a layer above the page, and inverting is the cheapest way to say so without a heavy shadow. Money colours are the dark-theme variants here, because the surface is dark even though the page is not.

What not to plot

No pie or donut for P&L composition. Angles are read badly and losses are negative — a negative slice is meaningless. Use a signed bar.

No dual y-axes. Two scales on one plot let any two series be made to look correlated.

No projections, no counterfactuals. This product shows what happened. A dotted line into the future is a different product, and one this system will not draw.
