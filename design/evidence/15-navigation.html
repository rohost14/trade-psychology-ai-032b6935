Navigation

One structure rendered two ways. Desktop gets a sidebar, mobile gets the same primary items in a bottom bar with the rest behind “More”. The grouping and the labels never differ between them — a screen that lives under Risk on one device cannot live somewhere else on the other.

Four primary items, then labelled groups. The active state is an accent tint with a 4px radius — not a pill, not a left bar. It reads as “selected” without becoming the loudest thing in the sidebar.

The alert count is the only place a solid red appears in navigation, and it is a count of things that already happened — not a decoration.

Nine items, four at the top. Past roughly seven, a flat list stops being scanned and starts being searched. Groups restore that by turning nine items into three sets of two or three.

The same four primary items, plus More for the two labelled groups. No raised centre button: a floating action button implies one dominant action per screen, and this product’s screens are for reading rather than doing.

Every target is at least 44px tall including its label, and the bar sits inside the safe area so the last row of content is never under a home indicator.

ink underline, not accent

Tabs underline in ink while the sidebar highlights in accent. They are different kinds of movement — the sidebar changes where you are, tabs change what you are looking at in the same place — and using one colour for both loses that distinction.
