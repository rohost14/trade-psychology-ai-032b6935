Voice

This product tells people uncomfortable things about themselves. The whole design positioning collapses if the writing gets it wrong — so the copy rules are part of the system, not a content guideline kept somewhere else.

Three rules

  • State, do not judge. The product reports what happened. It is a mirror, and a mirror that editorialises is a critic.
  • Never console, never congratulate. Both signal that the reader’s feelings are being managed, which is the fastest way to lose their trust in the numbers.
  • Attribute to behaviour, never to character. “You entered three trades in ten minutes” is observable. “You are impulsive” is a verdict on a person and cannot be evidenced.

Reporting a loss

Not this Ouch — tough day out there! 😔 Don’t worry, everyone has these. Tomorrow is a fresh start!
This You lost ₹12,400 today. ₹8,900 of it came from three trades taken within eleven minutes of a loss.

The first manages a feeling and reports nothing. The second is specific, attributable and useful — and it respects the reader enough to let them have their own reaction to it.

Naming a pattern

Not this You’re a revenge trader. This is destroying your account.
This Revenge trading — entering within five minutes of a loss greater than 2%. Three times this month, costing ₹12,400.

The pattern gets a definition and a measurement. That turns an accusation into a finding the reader can check, disagree with, or act on.

Delivering good news

Not this Amazing work! 🎉 You’re crushing it — keep up the great discipline!
This Four sessions without an impulsive entry. Your previous best was two.

Praise is as much of a distortion as scolding. The fact is the reward; if the streak is genuinely good, saying the number already says so.

When something fails

Not this Error 500: Internal server error. Something went wrong.
This We couldn’t load your rules. They are still saved and still being enforced — this is only a display problem.

Say what happened, whose problem it is, and what is safe. A trader who thinks their loss limits vanished will do something about it, and that something is worse than the outage.

The verdict form

This week

Your losses were not from bad setups. They were from what you did after one.

Trades taken more than thirty minutes after a loss won 61% of the time. Trades taken within five minutes won 22%.

One sentence, in the serif, then the evidence underneath in the sans. The claim is allowed to be pointed because the numbers under it are doing the arguing. A verdict with no figures beneath it is an opinion, and this product does not have opinions.

Words this product does not use

should, must, stop, don’t, avoid — instruction. The philosophy is mirror, not blocker.
could have, would have, missed out — counterfactuals. The product reports what happened, not what might have.
guarantee, proven, always, never — overclaiming about a market.
oops, whoops, uh-oh, no worries — softening bad news is the failure this whole system is designed against.
